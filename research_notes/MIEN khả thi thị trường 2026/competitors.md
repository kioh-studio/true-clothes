# Competitive landscape: consumer AI wardrobe / outfit / stylist apps (as of Oct 2026)

Method note: about 20 web searches/fetches. The search tool returned thin data. No Sensor Tower / Appfigures / AppMagic figures were reachable, so there are NO verified download or revenue numbers for any app below. Several sources are aggregators or competitor-written blogs (flagged). Items I could not source are in Gaps, not asserted.

## 1. Who are the active competitors, and what do they offer, charge, and how big are they?

### Takeaway
Funding and attention in 2025-26 moved to well-funded US AI-stylist/shopping startups (Alta, Doji, Phia). Legacy closet organizers (Whering, Indyx, Acloset) persist as free-or-cheap freemium. Google's standalone Doppl app is dead, with try-on folded into Search. No verified download/revenue data was found for any of them.

### Cited Findings
**Alta Daily (Flagship AI, Inc., US/New York)**
- Raised $11M seed led by Menlo Ventures, with Aglaé Ventures, Benchstrength, Conviction and Phenomenal Ventures; angels include Tony Xu, Manish Chandra, Karlie Kloss. Founder Jenny Wang, 28 at the time. Announced June 2025 — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/alta-raises-11-million-in-seed-funding-for-ai-powered-personal-shopping-styling-app/2025061782265), [Retailboss](https://retailboss.co/alta-raises-11-million-turn-closet-ai-stylist/). A Preqin profile dates the round April 2026, which conflicts with the June 2025 coverage ([Preqin](https://www.preqin.com/data/profile/asset/alta-daily/748273)).
- App Store (Oct 2026): rating 4.9, 16K ratings, version 2.2.5 (Oct 1), first version 1.0 dated 2025-03-13. Price: Free, no IAP listed. Features: weather/schedule outfits, closet add by email-receipt forwarding / Alta database / photo, background removal, cost-per-wear, packing lists, closet-gap shopping recommendations, wishlist with sale alerts, avatar virtual try-on, web app. Privacy label collects precise location, contact info, photos, search history, all linked to the user — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6481705400). The rating count was 5.4K in an earlier snapshot (Preqin page, undated) and 16K now, which suggests fast growth (inference).

**Whering (UK, founder/CEO Bianca Rangecroft)**
- Podcast descriptions claim more than 10M users globally; this is self-reported and undated — [Buzzsprout](https://techbrainstalk.buzzsprout.com/681950/10437758-how-to-successfully-scale-your-app-startup-while-bootstrapping-with-bianca-rangecroft).
- CB Insights lists only about $20K raised over 3 rounds, the latest a Google for Startups Accelerator: AI First on 2025-06-18; the page's other fields were placeholders — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/whering-1/financials).
- Whering's own FAQ says the app is still free to use — [Whering FAQ](https://whering.co.uk/faq/can-i-use-whering-if-i-dont-pay). A competitor-written blog says "free, no paid plans", with a public community; it lists no weather info and calls the design clunky. The blog sells a rival app, so it is biased — [fits-app.com](https://fits-app.com/posts/top-8-closet-outfit-planning-apps-reviewed).

**Indyx (US)**
- Pre-seed (undisclosed, Alante Capital, July 2022) per a low-authority profile — [yespress.io](https://yespress.io/indyx). The claim of 10M+ items catalogued is unconfirmed.
- Pricing: core styling-yourself is free. "Indyx Insider" is reportedly about $12.99/month or $74.99/year (analytics, flatlays, virtual try-on). Human stylist sessions start around $60. "The Feed" (weekly human-styled outfits) starts at $25/month. Source is an aggregated search summary plus the company blog; I did not confirm current prices — [Indyx blog](https://myindyx.com/blog/launching-the-feed?format=amp).

**Acloset (Looko, South Korea)**
- 2022 milestones: more than 1M global users within 20 months of launch, 95% global, largest markets US and Spain, 85% women aged 10s-20s. Korean users about 49K. Press page lists a $2.1M Series A. Dealroom shows ₩3.0B Series A — [Acloset press](https://acloset.app/press-acloset), [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/acloset).
- StartupHub revenue estimate of $20.1M (27% confidence) and funding of $63M conflict with the company's own figures; treat as unreliable — [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/startups/acloset.md).
- Korean App Store: 4.2 from 595 ratings; newer shopping advisor for items you do not own — [App Store KR](https://apps.apple.com/KR/app/id1542311809).
- Pricing: free up to 100 items, paid roughly $2.33-$24.99 (period unclear), per the competitor blog [fits-app.com](https://fits-app.com/posts/top-8-closet-outfit-planning-apps-reviewed).

**Doji (US)** — $14M seed led by Thrive Capital with Seven Seven Six, announced May 2025, days after its App Store launch. Co-founders Dorian Dargan and Jim Winkens. Avatar-based try-on. No a16z involvement found — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/15/doji-raises-14m-to-make-virtual-try-ons-fun-through-ai-avatars).

**Phia (US, Phoebe Gates / Sophia Kianni)** — price-comparison / AI shopping, not a closet app, but it competes for the shopping moment. About $30M at a roughly $180M valuation (Bloomberg via eWeek, Dec 2025); $35.5M Series A reported in 2026, with total funding $43.5M. It self-reports 1.5M+ users in about a year. A "$120M Series B at $850M" blog claim is uncorroborated and should not be used — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/de/news/ai-shopping-app-phia-funding/), [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/phia-raises-35-5-million-series-a-and-reveals-star-studded-investor-roster-to-expand-ai-powered-shopping-platform/amp/), [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.ca/news/business/phia-secures-35-million-dollars-in-series-a-funding-to-scale-ai-shopping-platform/2026012943466).

**Daydream / Onton** — an overview says Daydream launched June 2025 with over 2M products from about 8,500 brands. Onton reportedly grew from about 50K to over 2M monthly active shoppers by late 2025, starting in furniture. No funding found for either. Low-quality aggregator — [newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-shopping-top-startups).

**Aiuta (Cyprus-based, B2B)** — try-on infrastructure sold to retailers such as ASOS. Shopify app from $19.99/month. Funding is unclear (a "$16M Series A" headline conflicts with the seed 2023 date) — [Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/aiuta-try-on), [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/aiuta). It is B2B, so not a direct consumer rival.

**Minor closet apps (pricing from a competitor blog "Updated for 2026", unverified)** — [fits-app.com](https://fits-app.com/posts/top-8-closet-outfit-planning-apps-reviewed):
- Fits: free, membership $3.33/month.
- SimpleCloset: free to 30 items, from $1.49/month, iOS only.
- Smart Closet: $3.99 one-time plus $0.99/month cloud backup, "appears no longer maintained".
- GetWardrobe: free, paid from $2.90/month.
- Combyne: free, paid from $2.99/month, collage-focused.
- Pureple: free with full-screen ads.
- Stylebook: one-time payment, "not actively maintained for 3-5 years" (this is the blog's claim).

**Google Doppl** — Labs app launched June 2025 (US only, 18+, iOS and Android). Shut down on 2026-04-30, with try-on now inside Search/Shopping/Images. Confirmed by secondary sources only (a Japanese blog dated 2026-03-24 and others); I did not reach Google's own notice — [TechCrunch launch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/26/google-launches-doppl-a-new-app-that-lets-you-visualize-how-an-outfit-might-look-on-you), [Jetstream](https://jetstream.blog/2026/03/24/google-doppl-shut-down-end-of-april-2026/).

### Inferences
- The cheapest tier of the market (free to about $3/month) is crowded. Alta is also free, so a paid MIEN would be competing against free, funded rivals on price.
- Doppl's shutdown suggests a standalone try-on app has weak defensibility against platform features (Google Search try-on). That is a risk for MIEN's "AI try-on image" feature as a differentiator.

### Gaps
- No verified downloads/MAU/revenue (Sensor Tower, Appfigures, data.ai, AppMagic) for any app; not reachable.
- Not covered or no data found: Style DNA, OpenWardrobe, Getwardrobe usage, Pureple, Dressr, Save Your Wardrobe, Lookscope, Glance, Pinterest, Amazon features. Combyne and Stylebook only appear in the blog.
- Whering premium tier and price, team size for Whering/Indyx/Acloset/Alta, and Alta's real post-seed status.
- Google's own Doppl shutdown announcement.

## 2. Which competitors offer MIEN's distinguishing features?

### Takeaway
From what I verified, no competitor combined body-measurement fit scoring, pre-purchase verdict and gap analysis. Alta offers closet-gap shopping and avatar try-on, which is the closest overlap. On-device privacy appears only in a small new color-analysis app (Drape). I found nothing confirming body-measurement fit scoring in any wardrobe app (absence of evidence from limited search).

### Cited Findings
- Alta Daily: closet-gap-based shopping recommendations, wishlist, avatar try-on, cost-per-wear. Collects photos and precise location linked to the user, so no on-device privacy claim — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6481705400).
- Acloset: a shopping advisor recommending high-utility items the user does not own, which is a gap-analysis analogue — [App Store KR](https://apps.apple.com/KR/app/id1542311809).
- Indyx: virtual try-on listed under the paid Insider tier, plus human stylists — [Indyx blog](https://myindyx.com/blog/launching-the-feed?format=amp).
- StyleIt AI (SEEMFLOW): color analysis plus virtual draping, plus "outfit scoring from photos" with compatibility scores — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6670424652). This is partial overlap with MIEN's score, but it scores color, not fit.
- Drape: selfie analyzed on-device, 12-season palette, wardrobe scans, never uploaded (developer claim) — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/drape-color-analysis/6783804650).
- Dressika: 12-season colour analysis with virtual wardrobe and outfit colour matching — [App Store KR](https://apps.apple.com/kr/app/%ED%8D%BC%EC%8A%A4%EB%84%90%EC%BB%AC%EB%9F%AC-%EC%96%BC%EA%B5%B4-%EC%A7%84%EB%8B%A8-%EC%83%89%EC%83%81-%ED%8C%94%EB%A0%88%ED%8A%B8-dressika/id1536745174?l=en-GB).
- Phia: shopping comparison across 350M+ products (self-reported), not a closet — [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/phia-raises-35-5-million-series-a-and-reveals-star-studded-investor-roster-to-expand-ai-powered-shopping-platform/amp/).

### Inferences
- MIEN's closest unique combination is "in-shop verdict scored against owned wardrobe plus body measurements". I found no sourced competitor doing all of that, but my search was shallow, so verify before claiming it as a moat.
- The Wardrobe Critic idea is partly shadowed by Alta and Acloset, so frame it as depth (which one item unlocks the most outfits), not novelty.

### Gaps
- Did not check per-app for body-measurement fit features (e.g. Stitch Fix-style, ASOS Fit Assistant, Zalando size advice, Fits).
- No data on Pinterest or Amazon features. No evidence either way on "AI try-on with own wardrobe" beyond Alta and Indyx avatars.

## 3. Shutdowns, pivots, stalls

### Takeaway
Confirmed: Doppl shut down April 2026 and Mallzee ceased operations after pivoting to B2B. Others (Finery, Chicisimo, Closet+) had no sourced status in this pass. Cladwell's story could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- Doppl: shut down 2026-04-30, features moved into Google Search/Shopping — [Jetstream](https://jetstream.blog/2026/03/24/google-doppl-shut-down-end-of-april-2026/) (secondary).
- Mallzee (Edinburgh): swipe-style shopping app that pivoted to B2B consumer-preference insights; CB Insights lists it as ceased operations; date not found — [CB Insights compare](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/mallzee-vs-poshmark).
- Cladwell: a deal-tracking site says it was acquired by its co-founders (Erin and Colin Flynn) in August 2019. An undated local-news item says it "went bust" and later rebounded, reaching profitability and planning an "Ask Cladwell" feature. A follow-up search found nothing, so treat as unverified — [theygotacquired](https://theygotacquired.com/saas/cladwell-acquired/), [Mammoth Times](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/news/read/43521694/after_going_bust).
- Smart Closet and Stylebook are described as likely unmaintained by a competitor blog — [fits-app.com](https://fits-app.com/posts/top-8-closet-outfit-planning-apps-reviewed).

### Inferences
- Pattern: B2C closet apps without a subscription model or a shopping/affiliate engine tend to stall; the survivors either stayed free and community-led (Whering) or took VC money (Alta).

### Gaps
- Finery, Chicisimo, Closet+ status and causes: no sources found.
- Reasons for Doppl's shutdown come only from secondary commentary; Google's reasoning is not confirmed.

## 4. App Store / Google Play ratings and review themes

### Takeaway
Ratings are high for the new AI apps (Alta 4.9 on 16K) and mid for legacy ones (Acloset KR 4.2). Complaint themes are consistent: manual data-entry friction, poor background removal, paywalls after a handful of items, and instability. The review-theme sources are aggregators, not raw store reviews.

### Cited Findings
- Alta 4.9 / 16K ratings — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6481705400). Acloset KR 4.2 / 595 — [App Store KR](https://apps.apple.com/KR/app/id1542311809). StyleIt AI 4.3 / 351 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6670424652). Dressika (KR store) 4.4 / 104 — [App Store KR](https://apps.apple.com/kr/app/%ED%8D%BC%EC%8A%A4%EB%84%90%EC%BB%AC%EB%9F%AC-%EC%96%BC%EA%B5%B4-%EC%A7%84%EB%8B%A8-%EC%83%89%EC%83%81-%ED%8C%94%EB%A0%88%ED%8A%B8-dressika/id1536745174?l=en-GB).
- Whering Google Play feedback report: background-removal problems, slow performance, unexpected app closing — [Kimola](https://kimola.com/reports/whering-digital-closet-stylist-user-feedback-report-google-play-en-144486).
- Aggregator reports for other wardrobe apps: top complaint is the manual digitization friction, plus mandatory subscription after very few items (one user: nine items) and crashes. One source rates its own confidence 0.2/1.0 — [Marlvel](https://marlvel.ai/apps/my-wardrobe-outfit-closet), [chrome-stats](https://chrome-stats.com/d/id1463372370).
- Competitor-blog critiques: Acloset has a cluttered UI, 100-item free cap and inconsistent cut-outs; Indyx has slow one-by-one entry — [fits-app.com](https://fits-app.com/posts/top-8-closet-outfit-planning-apps-reviewed).

### Inferences
- MIEN's free on-device single-item cut-out directly targets the top two complaints (entry friction, cut-out quality), but only if its quality actually holds up.
- Alta's receipt-email import is a strong counter to data-entry burden that MIEN does not have (per the feature list given).

### Gaps
- No Google Play ratings found for any app. No Reddit threads were retrieved (r/capsulewardrobe etc.).
- Whering App Store rating not found.

## 5. Personal color analysis apps

### Takeaway
A crowded, low-priced niche with a mix of one-time unlocks and subscriptions. All traction data is missing, and the newest entrant (Drape, July 2026) is already ranked outside the US top 30 Lifestyle.

### Cited Findings
- Drape: first released 2026-07-05, free tier with one full analysis, one-time unlock for the full palette/makeup/metals/hair/unlimited wardrobe scans (no subscription), optional "Drape Plus" subscription for AI stylist chat; on-device single-selfie analysis, 12-season palette, olive undertone handling; rank outside top 30 US Free Lifestyle — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/drape-color-analysis/6783804650).
- StyleIt AI (SEEMFLOW): IAPs $5.99, $7.99, $19.99, $29.99 (plan periods not shown; one listed $0.00 lifetime item is unexplained); selfie uploaded, face photos "collected, not linked", usage data used to track across other companies' apps — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6670424652).
- Dressika (STANDY SOFT SL, Spain): 12 seasons, 120 clothing colours, 170 makeup shades, 180 hair colours; claims "up to 97% accuracy"; premium priced at ₩6,500 to ₩59,000 depending on plan, weekly from ₩6,400 — [App Store KR](https://apps.apple.com/kr/app/%ED%8D%BC%EC%8A%A4%EB%84%90%EC%BB%AC%EB%9F%AC-%EC%96%BC%EA%B5%B4-%EC%A7%84%EB%8B%A8-%EC%83%89%EC%83%81-%ED%8C%94%EB%A0%88%ED%8A%B8-dressika/id1536745174?l=en-GB).

### Inferences
- Color analysis is a commodity feature priced at a one-time $8-$30 or a few dollars/month. As a MIEN feature it adds acquisition appeal but not pricing power.
- Drape's on-device claim shows privacy is now a marketing angle in this niche, which supports MIEN's on-device story.

### Gaps
- Colorwise, Palette.fm, Jennie's: no data found. Downloads/revenue for Drape, StyleIt AI, Dressika: not found.
