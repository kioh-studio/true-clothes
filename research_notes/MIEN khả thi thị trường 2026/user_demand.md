# User demand, engagement and retention for digital wardrobe / AI stylist jobs (as of 2026-10)

Method note: ~17 web searches/fetches. Search tool indexes Reddit and App Store reviews poorly, so community sentiment is thin. Tracker/analyst figures are third-party estimates. No hard retention (D30/D90) figures for any wardrobe app were found.

## 1. Retention reality and the data-entry cliff (Whering, Indyx, Acloset, Stylebook)

### Takeaway
No wardrobe app has published retention (D30, D90, MAU/installs); only registered-user counts, which say nothing about continued use. Upfront cataloguing effort is the consistently reported friction; evidence of mitigation working is qualitative (reviewers say Whering's item library and Acloset's auto-tagging make onboarding easier), not quantified.

### Cited Findings
- Whering announced a $7M seed (eBay Ventures, Google AI Futures Fund) on 2026-07-07 and "10 million users globally"; the article gives no retention, engagement, items-per-user or revenue data. Funds go to personalised recommendations (weather, mood, occasion), image enhancement, gallery scanning (identify clothes from photos) and virtual try-on; resale integrations planned. — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/07/07/whering-lands-7m-as-digital-wardrobe-platform-reaches-10m-users/); one source says most are Gen Z — [Inforcapital summary via search](https://inforcapital.com/news/whering-lands-7m-as-digital-wardrobe-platform-reaches-10m-users/)
- Funding totals conflict: one report says ~$14M raised incl. this round, a database lists $7M. — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/07/07/whering-lands-7m-as-digital-wardrobe-platform-reaches-10m-users/), [Startup-Seeker](https://startup-seeker.com/company/whering~co~uk)
- Older baseline: Whering reportedly had 20,000 users in its first year (2021) — [FashionUnited, 2021 (older)](https://fashionunited.com/news/fashion/digital-wardrobe-app-whering-attracts-20-000-users-within-first-year/2021062940719). Implies ~10M registered users mostly came in 2023-2026 (inference, see below).
- Acloset (Looko Inc., Korea, founded 2020): 800,000+ users in 18 months, 95% global (Oct 2022, older); site now claims 8M users; Google Play listing mentions ~4M; free up to 100 items then subscription. Funding figures conflict ($2.42M incl. 2022 Series A per IdeaUsher vs "$63M" per StartupHub, which looks unreliable). — [KoreaTechDesk](https://koreatechdesk.com/korean-startup-lookos-ai-digital-wardrobe-app-acloset-gets-over-800000-global-users), [Acloset](https://www.acloset.app/), [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/acloset), [IdeaUsher](https://ideausher.com/blog/ai-fashion-app-like-acloset-development/), [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/startups/acloset)
- Good On You hands-on review (2024-06-26): Indyx has the heaviest cataloguing load (no auto-detect of category/colour, no bulk photo upload, slow loading); Whering easier because of a built-in library of common items; Acloset cataloguing "seamless" via automated tagging and background remover; Acloset engagement features: weather-based daily planning, auto outfit suggestions, ideas feed, multiple closets. — [Good On You](https://goodonyou.eco/wardrobe-organising-apps/)
- Indyx sells human cataloguing (up to 100 items for $295; its own estimate ~2 min/item, 4-6 hours for an average wardrobe) and advises "catalogue as you go". That a vendor sells cataloguing as a service is itself evidence the data-entry cliff is real. — [Indyx How it works](https://myindyx.com/how-it-works)
- Indyx business model: free self-styling; revenue from stylist marketplace, human-styled weekly "Feed" from $25/month, cataloguing service. One third-party review cites $5/mo and $15/mo tiers (single source, unverified). No user/subscriber counts found. — [Indyx Feed launch](https://myindyx.com/blog/launching-the-feed?format=amp), [Indyx FAQ](https://www.myindyx.com/blog/how-digital-styling-works-frequently-asked-questions), [GetWardrobe comparison](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/indyx/)
- Anecdote: users catalogue in stages (e.g. cold-weather clothes first); a blogger moved from Whering to Indyx after logging everything in Whering (i.e. migration cost is tolerated by enthusiasts). — [Indyx Substack comments](https://indyxapp.substack.com/p/the-state-of-our-wardrobes-isconcerning/comments), [curated, prn](https://curatedprn.substack.com/p/lessons-from-digitizing-my-wardrobe)
- Generic benchmark (not wardrobe-specific): average mobile app D1 retention ~25%, D30 ~5.7% (Adjust 2024 data, cited in 2026 blog). — [Sonar blog](https://trysonar.app/blog/app-user-retention-benchmarks-and-what-moves-it.md)

### Inferences
- Registered-user counts (Whering 10M, Acloset 4-8M) are cumulative downloads/sign-ups; with typical lifestyle-app decay, active users are likely a small fraction. No data confirms or refutes this.
- Mitigations seen in market: preloaded item library (Whering), auto-tagging + background removal (Acloset), human cataloguing (Indyx), and now gallery/photo scanning (Whering 2026 roadmap). Whering using investor money for gallery scanning suggests onboarding remains an unsolved bottleneck.
- Email-receipt and screenshot import: no evidence found of effectiveness for any wardrobe app.

### Gaps
- No published D30/D90 retention, DAU/MAU, items-per-user, or paid conversion for Whering, Indyx, Acloset, Stylebook.
- No data on email-receipt import (e.g. via Gmail) or screenshot import adoption or effect on retention.
- Stylebook user/retention data not found.

## 2. Review and community sentiment

### Takeaway
Weak evidence only. Enthusiast anecdotes are positive about the audit insight (spotting repeats/never-worn, cost-per-wear) and fun swipe features; complaints cluster on effort, slowness, and paywalls. I could not retrieve Reddit threads or App Store review corpora.

### Cited Findings
- Reviewer found cataloguing "calming" and revealing of repeats/never-worn items. — [curated, prn](https://curatedprn.substack.com/p/lessons-from-digitizing-my-wardrobe)
- Bloggers cite Reddit discussion of cost-per-wear tracking as what led them to Stylebook/Indyx/Whering (use case is CPW tracking, not daily styling). — [Does this look weird? Substack](https://doesthislookweird.substack.com/p/digitising-my-wardrobe-using-whering)
- Whering's "Dress Me" swipe feature described as fun; Acloset's marketplace criticised for no seller reviews. — [Good On You](https://goodonyou.eco/wardrobe-organising-apps/)
- Style DNA (AI stylist/colour app) reviews: many call recommendations generic and not worth the high subscription price (aggregator summary). — [Apptail](https://apptail.io/app/style-dna-ai-color-analysis-soT/united-states)

### Inferences
- Comparison sites (Indyx, Whering) are commercially interested; positive claims are low-weight.
- Value seems to come from insight/audit moments and CPW, rather than daily "what to wear" habit, but this is unproven.

### Gaps
- No Reddit (r/femalefashionadvice, r/capsulewardrobe, r/malefashionadvice, r/minimalism) or App Store/Play review theme analysis obtained; needs direct Reddit/review scraping.

## 3. Pre-purchase decisions: returns, fit, unworn clothes, WTP

### Takeaway
Fit is the strongest hard-data pain point: US online apparel return rate ~23.4% (2025) with ~70% of returners citing size/fit. Unworn-clothes data is mostly older (2018) and survey-inconsistent. No direct willingness-to-pay evidence for "should I buy this" was found.

### Cited Findings
- Coresight Research: US online apparel return rate 23.4% in 2025; returned merchandise ~$47.1 (unit omitted in FashionUnited, likely billions) on a $201.1B online apparel+footwear market; nearly 70% of shoppers who returned online clothing cited size and fit. Source is Coresight/Alvanon "Shifting the Size and Fit Paradigm" (May 2026), sponsored partner content. — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/sizing-intelligence-is-strategic-priority-as-brands-prepare-for-ai-driven-commerce/2026063073231), [NAUMD summary](https://naumd.com/new-report-shifting-the-size-and-fit-paradigm/)
- Coresight Dec 2025: 58% of US consumers familiar with AI had used or intended to use AI tools for shopping; ~40% abandoned an online apparel purchase due to confusing/missing product information. — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/sizing-intelligence-is-strategic-priority-as-brands-prepare-for-ai-driven-commerce/2026063073231)
- Loop Returns (2024): 23.2% adjusted apparel return rate, 22M returns, 4,000+ Shopify merchants. McKinsey (2021, older): ~70% of apparel returns from poor fit or style. — [Size.ly return stats (secondary)](https://www.size.ly/blog/ecommerce-return-statistics)
- Unworn clothes: Movinga 2018 (older): 18,000 households, 20 countries; actual unworn in last 12 months far above self-estimates (US 82%, Belgium 88%; Belgians estimated 26%). WeightWatchers UK 2018 (older): women wear ~55% of clothing. WRAP (via secondary blog, date unclear): ~118 items per person, ~26% unworn in past year. Aalborg 2025 study: 63% of participants' least-worn dresses never worn (narrow sample). — [FashionUnited 2018](https://fashionunited.com/news/fashion/people-do-not-wear-at-least-50-percent-of-their-wardrobes-says-study/2018081622868), [Latico Leathers (secondary)](https://laticoleathers.com/blogs/resources/fashion-consumption-trends-ownership-use-and-lifespan), [PLATE 2025](https://journals.aau.dk/index.php/plate2025/article/download/10427/8323/34158)

### Inferences
- Returns/fit data show the pain exists at retailer level (retailers pay), but a consumer paying to avoid it is not demonstrated. B2B (retailer size tools) is where fit money flows per this evidence.
- The unworn figures vary 26%-82% depending on definition; cite as a range, flag 2018 ones as old.

### Gaps
- No consumer survey on regret purchases or WTP for a "should I buy" verdict found.
- No data on in-store scan/verdict app usage.
- Primary Coresight/Alvanon report not read; Narvar/NRF 2025 return data not retrieved.

## 4. Personal color analysis demand

### Takeaway
PCA is a clearly viral, tourism-linked Gen Z trend (Seoul), with in-person sessions ~$115-300, but reliable market-size and app-revenue data is scarce. App evidence points to a low-priced, mostly one-time need (one-time $14.99 pricing is common) with high download counts but generic-feeling subscription complaints.

### Cited Findings
- Seoul sessions priced roughly 150,000-400,000 KRW (~$115-300); Hongdae averages ~193,000-385,000 KRW; trend driven by TikTok/Instagram since ~2022; Seoul city marketed it to tourists; appointments hard to get. — [Sonagi](https://sonagi.co.uk/blogs/blog/korea-s-viral-personal-colour-analysis-phenomenon), [Curly Tales](https://curlytales.com/south-korea-seoul-entices-gen-z-tourists-with-viral-personal-colour-analysis-deets-inside/amp/), [Korea Experience](https://koreaexperience.com/blog/personal-color-analysis-hongdae-2026)
- Korea personalised cosmetics market $1.1B (2021) projected $4B by 2025 (broad category, not PCA). — [JoongAng via Daum](https://v.daum.net/v/GbpoaShVOi?f=p)
- Apps: Colorwise/My Best Colors 1M+ Google Play downloads, 4.1 stars, free ad-supported with one-time ~$14.99 upgrade; Dressika 5M+ Play downloads; Style DNA 2.5M+ downloads, est. ~$20K/month revenue from ~1K monthly downloads (third-party tracker, rough). — [Hathaura](https://www.hathaura.co/blog/best-color-analysis-apps), [Apptail](https://apptail.io/app/style-dna-ai-color-analysis-soT/united-states), [Play: My Best Colors](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.colorwise.me.allseasons&hl=en_US)
- Unreliable (do not use without verification): "375 million TikTok views" (Sportskeeda, unverifiable), "368% increase" (Korea Experience, unsourced). I found no app named "Drape" in results.

### Inferences
- Season/palette is a stable trait, so the core need is one-off (diagnosis), with recurrence only via application (shopping, outfit matching). One-time pricing in the category supports this.
- A free PCA as an acquisition hook is consistent with the category, paid retention must come from other jobs.

### Gaps
- No credible market size for PCA, no TikTok view counts from a primary source, no data on repeat usage.

## 5. Body measurement / fit trust and privacy

### Takeaway
Academic evidence consistently shows privacy concern reduces adoption of body scanning, and control/transparency mitigates it; no quantified survey of trust in camera-based measurement or size-tool adoption rates found.

### Cited Findings
- Youn et al. 2023 (n=285): body-information privacy concerns negatively influence future adoption of 3D body scanning; perceived control and responsiveness mitigate. — [Humanities & Social Sciences Communications](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v10y2023i1d10.1057_s41599-023-01632-y.html)
- Saudi study (n=82 scanned women): strong positive reaction to scan, but most worried about privacy; size prediction and virtual try-on had strongest market potential. — [Consumer experience of 3D body scanning](https://sindex.sdl.edu.sa/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Consumer-experience-of-3D-body-scanning/9949641508331)
- Coresight: retailers "increasingly adopting" predictive sizing; no adoption rate given. — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/sizing-intelligence-is-strategic-priority-as-brands-prepare-for-ai-driven-commerce/2026063073231)
- Vendor claim (marketing, unverified): Kleep body-scan sizing live on Lacoste's storefront. — [Kleep](https://kleep.ai/blog/how-body-scan-sizing-works)

### Inferences
- MIEN's on-device capture is a positioning answer to the documented privacy-adoption barrier (consistent with the research, not proven to convert).

### Gaps
- No consumer survey on trust in photo-based measurement accuracy; no size-tool adoption/conversion statistics; no Asia/Vietnam data.

## 6. Market sizing and demographics

### Takeaway
Analyst estimates for the "digital wardrobe/wardrobe app" market span roughly $0.2B-$1.2B with low-quality methodology; user-count claims from leaders (Whering 10M, Acloset 4-8M) are more informative than analyst TAM. Demographics: Gen Z skew confirmed only for Whering (one source).

### Cited Findings
- Wardrobe app market: ~$224M (2024) to $399M by 2032 (Intel Market Research); ~$250M (2025) to $557M by 2034 (same publisher); digital wardrobe $364M (2024) to $647M by 2031 (QYResearch via Foriio); capsule wardrobe apps ~$631M (2024), 32.84% CAGR (Zion); digital wardrobe platform ~$1.2B (2025) to $4.7B by 2034 (MarketIntelo, least rigorous). — [Intel](https://www.intelmarketresearch.com/wardrobe-app-2025-2032-667-1709), [Foriio/QY](https://foriio.com/works/2251664), [Zion](https://www.zionmarketresearch.com/report/capsule-wardrobe-app-market), [MarketIntelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/digital-wardrobe-platform-market)
- Whering mostly Gen Z (see above). Men's share: not found.

### Inferences
- Treat paid report TAMs as order-of-magnitude (hundreds of $M); disagreement of 5x between publishers signals no reliable baseline.

### Gaps
- No gender split, no revenue for Whering/Indyx/Acloset, no 2023-2026 growth series from credible source.

## 7. Sustainability / "buy less" motive and conversion to paying

### Takeaway
Secondhand growth is strong and hard-data-backed, but evidence that the "shop your closet" motive converts to wardrobe-app payments was not found; monetisation in the sector is through resale, stylists and services.

### Cited Findings
- ThredUp 14th Resale Report (April 2026; GlobalData; n=3,268 US adults): US secondhand apparel grew 19% in 2025 (fastest since 2021, ~4x broader clothing retail); 59% of consumers bought secondhand in 2025 (+7 pts in 3 years); US market to $78.8B by 2030, global $393B; Gen Z/Millennials >70% of growth. — [ThredUp newsroom](https://newsroom.thredup.com/news/thredup-14th-resale-report), [Retail Dive](https://retaildive.com/news/us-resale-market-surpass-78-billion-in-2030-secondhand/816470/)
- Wardrobe-app monetisation signals: Whering raised from eBay Ventures (resale integration), Indyx offers resale + stylists, Acloset has a resale marketplace and 100-item free cap. — sources above.
- ThredUp is ThredUp's own marketing data (commercially interested).

### Inferences
- Resale-linked apps get investor interest (eBay lead), suggesting the commercially proven adjacent job is "sell/declutter what I own", not "style what I own".

### Gaps
- No survey linking sustainability attitudes to paid wardrobe-app use; McKinsey State of Fashion 2025/26 and Vogue Business/BoF not retrieved.

## Cross-job summary (analyst view, flagged as inference)
- (1) What to wear today: demand plausible (weather/daily planner features are what all leaders ship) but no retention proof; blocked by cataloguing cliff.
- (2) Should I buy: fit-driven returns are hard-data large (~23% return rate, ~70% fit/size), but evidence of consumer WTP is absent.
- (3) Wardrobe gaps: no direct evidence found either way.
- (4) Colors: strong viral interest, price anchor $115-300 in-person, likely one-time.
- (5) Size/fit: biggest quantified pain; privacy a documented barrier.
